**Premise correction:** The question assumes webhook delivery is backed by an external message broker (NATS or RabbitMQ). Neither is used — in fact, there is no external broker of any kind. A dependency scan of `go.mod`/`go.sum` finds no NATS, RabbitMQ, Redis, Kafka, or similar queueing library.

**Actual mechanism:** This is the `usememos/memos` Go project, and webhook delivery runs entirely in-process on a buffered Go channel with a small pool of goroutine workers.

- **Queue declaration:** `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39` — `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)`, a buffered channel (capacity 128) that *is* the queue.
- **Workers:** `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55` — the package `init()` spawns 4 goroutines, each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post(payload)` to do a direct HTTP POST.
- **Producer/enqueue:** `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196` — `PostAsync` does a non-blocking channel send and *drops* the delivery (logs a warning) if the queue is full. Producers live in `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go` (e.g. lines 43, 71).

Note the characteristics that follow from this design: the queue is in-memory only (does not survive restarts, not shared across instances) and is lossy under load (full-queue sends are dropped with no persistence or retry). Only the webhook *endpoint configuration* is stored in the DB (via `store.GetUserWebhooks`); the delivery queue itself is purely the Go channel above.
