**Mechanism: explicit Go code** (not a database-level cascade rule).

When a user is deleted, their emoji reactions are removed by explicit `DELETE FROM reaction` statements issued inside the delete-user transaction, in each driver's `deleteUserTargetsTx`. Two distinct deletions cover reactions:
- `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` — deletes reactions the user *authored* (`WHERE creator_id = ?`).
- `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` — deletes reactions *on the user's own memos* (`WHERE content_id IN (...)`, content IDs derived from the user's memo tree).

What I checked:

- **SQLite driver** — `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` and `:102` call the two helpers from `deleteUserTargetsTx`; the SQL is at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:383` (`DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN ...`) and `:401` (`DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ...`).
- **Postgres driver** — same wiring at `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96`/`:102`, SQL at `:383` and `:401`.
- **MySQL driver** — same wiring at `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96`/`:102`, SQL at `:420` and `:438`.
- **Schema (ruling out a DB-level rule)** — the `reaction` table definition has no foreign keys / `ON DELETE CASCADE`; it just stores a bare `creator_id INTEGER NOT NULL` and `content_id TEXT NOT NULL` with a uniqueness constraint (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`, and the mysql equivalent). So nothing removes reactions at the database level — the explicit Go statements are required.

Answer: **explicit Go code; deletions in each driver's `deleteUserTargetsTx` — `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96,102` (SQL at :383, :401), `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96,102` (SQL at :383, :401), `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96,102` (SQL at :420, :438); no FK/cascade in the `reaction` schema (`store/migration/{sqlite,postgres,mysql}/LATEST.sql`).**
