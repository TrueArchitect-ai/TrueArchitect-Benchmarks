**Premise correction: neither. The webhook delivery workers do not consume from any external message broker — there is no NATS, RabbitMQ, AMQP, or Kafka in this project.** A repo-wide search finds zero references to `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, or `kafka` in the Go source, and no such dependency in `go.mod`.

**Actual mechanism — an in-process buffered Go channel.** Webhook delivery is queued and consumed entirely within the server process:

- The "queue" is a buffered channel declared in the `webhook` package's package-level vars: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39` (capacity 128, in-memory only).

- The "workers" are 4 goroutines started in the package `init()` that range over that channel and call `Post` for each payload: `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55` (`for range 4 { go func() { for payload := range asyncPostQueue { Post(payload) } }() }`).

- Producers enqueue via `PostAsync`, which does a non-blocking send onto the channel and **drops the delivery if the buffer is full** (logging a warning) rather than blocking or persisting: `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-195` (send at `:190`).

Implications of this being an in-process channel rather than a broker: the queue is not durable (pending deliveries are lost on restart), not shared across instances, and bounded at 128 with best-effort drop-on-overflow. So the question's framing is incorrect — there's no broker to name and no externally-declared queue; it's a `chan *WebhookRequestPayload` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`.
