**Delivery function: `Post` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`; protection mechanism: SSRF guard via `safeDialContext`/`isReservedIP` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59` and `internal/webhook/validate.go:44`**

How it works:

- **Delivery:** `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120-180`) marshals the payload, optionally adds Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 signature headers, and executes the HTTP POST via the shared `safeClient`. Callers usually go through `PostAsync` (`webhook.go:184`), which enqueues onto a bounded channel serviced by 4 worker goroutines (`webhook.go:42-55`) that in turn call `Post`. It's triggered from memo change paths like `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go`.
- **Protection:** Two layers, both keyed off the reserved-range check `isReservedIP` (`internal/webhook/validate.go:44-54`, ranges defined at `validate.go:14-23` — loopback, RFC-1918, link-local/IMDS 169.254.0.0/16, and IPv6 equivalents):
  1. At registration time, `ValidateURL` (`validate.go:62-83`) rejects URLs that aren't http/https or that resolve to a reserved/private IP.
  2. At delivery time — the real enforcement — `safeClient`'s transport uses `safeDialContext` (`webhook.go:59-77`), which re-resolves the hostname at dial time and refuses to connect if any resolved address is reserved/private. Doing the check at dial time also defeats DNS-rebinding attacks that would pass the registration-time check.
- An escape hatch `AllowPrivateIPs` (`validate.go:41`) disables the guard for self-hosted deployments that intentionally target LAN services.
