Two separate corrections:

**1. No cleanup job exists.** Nothing in the codebase performs a periodic database cleanup, vacuum, prune, or purge. The only two background runners are in `server/runner/`: the s3presign runner (re-signs S3 attachment URLs every 12 hours, `server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:27`) and the memopayload runner (dead code — never constructed). Deletion work is all synchronous and event-driven: attachment storage cleanup happens inline during user deletion (`server/router/api/v1/user_service.go:421-450`), and memo deletion cascades in `store.DeleteMemo` (`server/router/api/v1/memo_service.go:579`).

**2. The cron scheduler is unused.** `internal/scheduler` is a full cron implementation — 5-field expression parser (`internal/scheduler/parser.go`), job registration and lifecycle (`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go:70`, `:91`, `:169`), plus recovery/logging middleware (`internal/scheduler/middleware.go`). But its only importers are its own tests: `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:10` and `internal/scheduler/integration_test.go:13`. No production code imports the package, so `scheduler.New` is never called and no job is ever registered or scheduled. The s3presign runner doesn't use it either — it drives itself off a plain `time.Ticker` (`server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:30`), wired directly into startup at `server/server.go:162-171`.

The nearest thing to a recurring "cleanup" on a timer is the in-memory cache eviction sweep, which runs every 5 minutes and touches no database (`store/store.go:42`, `store/cache/cache.go:83`, `:118`).
