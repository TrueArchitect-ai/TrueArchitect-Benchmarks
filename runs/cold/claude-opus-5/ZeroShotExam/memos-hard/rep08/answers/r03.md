The premise doesn't hold: there is no nightly database-cleanup job in this repository's cron scheduler.

What actually exists:

- `internal/scheduler/` is a **standalone, general-purpose cron library** — a scheduler engine (`scheduler.go`), cron parser (`parser.go`), job type (`job.go`), and middleware (`middleware.go`). It has no knowledge of the database.
- **Nothing in the codebase registers any job with it.** The only non-test `Register(...)` occurrence is inside a doc comment (`internal/scheduler/doc.go:14`); a grep for `internal/scheduler` imports outside the package itself returns only its own tests. So the scheduler is currently unused by the server.
- The "2 AM" figure you may be thinking of comes from **illustrative examples, not a real job**:
  - `internal/scheduler/doc.go:15-16` — a package-doc snippet naming a job `"daily-cleanup"` with `Schedule: "0 2 * * *", // 2 AM daily`, whose handler body is the comment `// Your cleanup logic here`.
  - `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:97-106` — `Example_multipleJobs` registers a job named `"cleanup"` at `"0 2 * * *"` that just prints `"Cleaning up old data..."`. The same example also has a health check at `*/5 * * * *` and a weekly backup at `0 1 * * 0`.

Neither is database-related, and neither runs in production.

The only actual recurring background work in the server is interval-based, not cron-based: `server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:30` uses a `time.NewTicker(runnerInterval)`, and `server/runner/memopayload/runner.go` is a one-shot migration-style runner.
