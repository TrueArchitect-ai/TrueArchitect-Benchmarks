**Premise correction: there is no nightly database-cleanup job in this repository.** The cron scheduler (`internal/scheduler/`) is a standalone, unused library — no production code registers any job with it.

What I found:

- `internal/scheduler/` implements a generic cron scheduler (parser, job registry, middleware), but the only importers of `github.com/usememos/memos/internal/scheduler` are its own tests: `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:10` and `internal/scheduler/integration_test.go:13`. Nothing in `cmd/`, `server/`, or `store/` wires it up, so no job is actually scheduled at runtime.
- The "cleanup at 2 AM" figure that likely prompted the question comes from *illustrative documentation and example code*, not a real job:
  - `internal/scheduler/doc.go:15-16` — package doc example: `Name: "daily-cleanup", Schedule: "0 2 * * *", // 2 AM daily`, with a `// Your cleanup logic here` stub handler.
  - `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:97-106` — `Example_multipleJobs` registers `"cleanup"` at `"0 2 * * *"` whose handler just prints "Cleaning up old data...".
  - `internal/scheduler/README.md:241-246` — a best-practices snippet showing a `"stale-session-cleanup"` job at `"0 3 * * *"` calling an undefined `cleanupSessions`.
  - None of these touch the database; they're print statements and stubs.

The actual periodic cleanup that exists in the codebase is unrelated to cron: the in-memory store cache runs a `time.Ticker` loop every 5 minutes (`store/cache/cache.go:46-47`, `199-218`, configured at `store/store.go:42`) to evict expired entries. That's cache eviction, not database cleanup, and it isn't scheduled by the cron scheduler.
