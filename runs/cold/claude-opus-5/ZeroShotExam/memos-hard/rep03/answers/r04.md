**Premise correction:** Neither. This repo (Memos, a single-binary Go app — `go.mod:1`) has no external message broker at all. Grepping `go.mod`/`go.sum` for `nats`, `rabbit`, `amqp`, `kafka`, `redis` returns nothing, and there is no broker service in the deployment docs. (The one repo-wide text match for "nats" is a Catalan translation string in `web/src/locales/ca.json`, not a dependency.)

**Actual mechanism — an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed goroutine worker pool**, all inside `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- **Queue declaration** — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`:
  ```go
  asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)
  ```
  A package-level buffered channel holding 128 payloads. That's the entire "queue."

- **Workers** — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`: a package `init()` spawns exactly 4 goroutines, each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post()` on every payload, logging a warning on failure. They live for the process lifetime; there's no shutdown or drain path.

- **Producer** — `PostAsync()` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196` does a non-blocking `select` send; if the buffer is full it hits the `default` branch and **drops the delivery** with a warning log (`webhook.go:191-195`). Callers are `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71` (memo created/updated/deleted and comment-created events).

- **Delivery** — `Post()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120-180`) does a single HTTP POST via a shared client with a 30s timeout and an SSRF-guarding dialer (`webhook.go:32-37`, `safeDialContext` at `:59`), optionally adding Standard Webhooks HMAC headers (`webhook-id`/`webhook-timestamp`/`webhook-signature`, `:133-150`).

Practical consequences of it being a channel rather than a broker: no durability (queued webhooks are lost on restart), no retries or dead-lettering (one attempt per payload), no cross-instance sharing, and silent drops under burst load beyond 128 in-flight items.
