**Explicit Go code — reactions are deleted by two SQL statements issued inside each driver's `DeleteUser` transaction; there is no database-level cascade for them.**

What I checked and found:

**1. The delete path.** `store.DeleteUser` (`store/user.go:171`) just delegates to `s.driver.DeleteUser` and then invalidates caches. The real work is per-driver, and each driver runs the whole cleanup in one transaction (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:29-59`).

**2. The explicit reaction deletions.** `deleteUserTargetsTx` calls two distinct reaction cleanups (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` and `:102`):

- `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:380`) runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`, removing reactions *others left on the deleted user's memos*. The content IDs are synthesized as `"memos/" + memo.UID` by `memoContentIDsFromRefs` (line 512), because `reaction.content_id` is an opaque string rather than a memo foreign key.
- `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:400`) runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`, removing reactions *the deleted user left on anyone's content*.

Both are mirrored in the other two drivers: `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:420` and `:438`, and `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:383` and `:401`.

**3. Why it can't be the database.** The `reaction` table has no foreign keys at all — only a `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)` constraint — in all three schemas: `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91-98`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`. Grepping for `REFERENCES`/`ON DELETE` across the three `LATEST.sql` files returns exactly one hit per dialect, and it belongs to `memo_share.memo_id` (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:109`), not to `reaction`. So an `ON DELETE CASCADE` cleanup is impossible here.

**4. Test confirmation.** `store/test/user_delete_test.go` seeds three reactions — a peer's reaction on the deleted user's memo (line 97), the deleted user's reaction on a peer memo (line 103), and a peer-on-peer reaction that should survive (line 109) — then after `DeleteUser` asserts that only the last one remains (lines 216-220). That covers both deletion directions and verifies unrelated reactions are preserved.
