**`(*renderer).addArg` — `internal/filter/render.go:842` — ~28 inbound calls (28 call sites from 14 distinct functions, all in production code)**

`addArg` is the argument-accumulator for the filter-to-SQL renderer: it bumps a placeholder counter, appends the value to `r.args`, and returns either `$N` for Postgres or `?` for the other dialects. Essentially every SQL fragment the renderer emits needs a bound parameter, which is why so much of the package funnels into it. All 28 call sites live in `internal/filter/render.go`, spread across 14 enclosing functions — the heaviest being `renderTagInList` (6 calls), plus `renderElementInCondition` and `foldedLike` (3 each), and `renderTagStartsWith`, `renderScalarInCondition`, `renderScalarComparison`, `renderRegex`, and `buildJSONArrayLike` (2 each). Notably `addBoolArg` (line 851) also delegates to it, so the true dynamic fan-in is a bit higher than the static count.

Method to get here: I enumerated all 370 function/method names declared under `internal/` (excluding `_test.go`), then counted call sites with scoping rules — package-qualified `pkg.Name(` for exported functions, `.Name(` for methods, and same-package-only matching for unexported functions — and excluded declaration lines. I also checked the four aliased internal imports (`mast`, `mparser`, `sttopenai`, `audiollmgemini`) to make sure aliasing wasn't hiding calls; they account for only six calls total. Since this is textual rather than type-resolved, method counts can absorb same-named methods from other types: the apparent top hit was `Next` at 55, but nearly all of those are `rows.Next()` from `database/sql`, and the scheduler's `Schedule.Next` has just one production caller (`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go:142`). `addArg` is immune to that problem — the name is unique to this package.
