**What I checked**

Driver parity: the same two functions are called at the same points, and defined identically, in `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go` (calls at lines 96 and 102; definitions at 380 and 400) and `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go` (calls at 96 and 102; definitions at 417 and 437). Each driver's `DeleteUser` wraps everything in one transaction that rolls back on error (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go` lines 29-59).

Schema: the `reaction` table has no foreign key and no `ON DELETE` clause in any dialect — see `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql` lines 92-99, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql` lines 91-98, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql` lines 91-98, and the original `store/migration/*/0.20/00__reaction.sql`. The only cascade left in the current schema is `memo_share.memo_id → memo(id)` (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql` line 109). Foreign keys were stripped from SQLite in `store/migration/sqlite/0.7/00__remove_fk.sql`, and the SQLite connection opens with `_pragma=foreign_keys(0)` (`store/db/sqlite/sqlite.go` line 53), so even a declared cascade wouldn't fire there.

Call path: `store.Store.DeleteUser` just delegates to the driver and clears caches (`store/user.go` lines 171-181); the API handler at `server/router/api/v1/user_service.go` lines 393-419 does permission checks and then relies on the driver transaction, doing no reaction cleanup of its own.

Test evidence: `store/test/user_delete_test.go` seeds three reactions — one by the peer on the deleted user's memo, one by the deleted user on the peer's memo, and one by the peer on the peer's own memo (lines 97-114) — then asserts after deletion that only the third survives (lines 216-220).
