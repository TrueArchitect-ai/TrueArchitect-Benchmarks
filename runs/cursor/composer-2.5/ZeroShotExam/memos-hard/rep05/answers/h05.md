**Evidence locations:**

1. **Entry point** — `server/router/api/v1/user_service.go` (`DeleteUser`) calls `s.Store.DeleteUser(...)`.

2. **Store layer** — `store/user.go` delegates to the DB driver’s `DeleteUser`.

3. **Explicit reaction deletes (all three backends)** — in each `store/db/{sqlite,postgres,mysql}/user_delete.go`, `deleteUserTargetsTx` runs two cleanup steps inside the delete-user transaction:
   - `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)` for memos being removed
   - `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?` for reactions the deleted user created on other users’ memos

   Example (SQLite): `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go` lines 96–104, 380–402.

4. **No DB-level cascade** — the `reaction` table schema has no `FOREIGN KEY`, `ON DELETE`, or trigger:
   - `store/migration/sqlite/0.20/00__reaction.sql`
   - `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql` (lines 91–99)
   - Same pattern in `store/migration/postgres/` and `store/migration/mysql/`

5. **Tests** — `store/test/user_delete_test.go` (`TestDeleteUserCleansRelatedData`) creates reactions, deletes the user, and asserts the deleted user’s reaction is gone while an unrelated peer reaction on a kept memo remains (lines 97–114, 216–220).
