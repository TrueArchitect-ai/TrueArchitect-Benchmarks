**Delivery function: `Post` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`; protection mechanism: dial-time SSRF guard `safeDialContext` blocking reserved/private IPs — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59` (with the reserved-range check `isReservedIP` in `internal/webhook/validate.go:44`)**

Details:

- Outbound webhook delivery executes in `webhook.Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120-180`), which marshals the payload, optionally adds Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 signature headers, and sends the POST through the shared `safeClient`. Asynchronous dispatches enter via `PostAsync` (`webhook.go:184`), which enqueues onto a bounded channel drained by four worker goroutines that call `Post` (`webhook.go:42-55`).
- The SSRF protection lives in the HTTP client's transport: `safeClient` uses `safeDialContext` as its `DialContext` (`webhook.go:32-37`). At dial time it resolves the target host and refuses the connection if any resolved IP falls in a reserved/private range (`webhook.go:59-77`) — loopback, RFC-1918 ranges, link-local/cloud-IMDS (169.254.0.0/16), and IPv6 equivalents, as defined in `reservedCIDRs` in `internal/webhook/validate.go:14-23`. Enforcing this at dial time also defeats DNS-rebinding tricks. A complementary early check, `ValidateURL` (`validate.go:62`), rejects such URLs when a webhook is created/updated, and the `AllowPrivateIPs` flag (`validate.go:41`) can disable the guard for self-hosted deployments that intentionally target local-network services.
