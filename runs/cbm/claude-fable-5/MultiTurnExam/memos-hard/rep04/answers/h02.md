**Delivery function: `Post` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`; protection mechanism: dial-time reserved/private-IP blocking via `safeDialContext` / `isReservedIP` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59` and `internal/webhook/validate.go:44`**

Details:

- **Where delivery executes:** `Post` in `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120-180` marshals the payload, optionally adds Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 signature headers, and sends the POST through the shared `safeClient`. Callers (e.g. the memo service via `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go`) usually go through `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184`), which enqueues onto a bounded channel drained by 4 worker goroutines (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`) that each ultimately call `Post`.
- **SSRF protection:** the HTTP client's transport uses a custom `DialContext` — `safeDialContext` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:59-77`) — which resolves the hostname and refuses to connect if any resolved IP falls in a reserved range (`isReservedIP`, `internal/webhook/validate.go:44-54`, backed by the `reservedCIDRs` list at `internal/webhook/validate.go:14-23`: loopback, RFC-1918, link-local/cloud-IMDS, and IPv6 equivalents). Because the check happens at dial time, it also defeats DNS-rebinding tricks. As a first line of defense, webhook URLs are also rejected at registration time by `ValidateURL` (`internal/webhook/validate.go:62`), and an `AllowPrivateIPs` flag (`internal/webhook/validate.go:41`) exists to opt out for self-hosted LAN deployments.
