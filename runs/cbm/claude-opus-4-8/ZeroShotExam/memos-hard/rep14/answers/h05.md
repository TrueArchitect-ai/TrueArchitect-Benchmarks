**Mechanism: removed by explicit Go code** (inside the DeleteUser transaction), not by any database-level cascade rule.

When a user is deleted, the store driver runs a transaction that explicitly deletes reactions in two ways:
1. Reactions **on the deleted user's content** — `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` issues `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)` for the user's memos (and comment-tree descendants).
2. Reactions **authored by the deleted user** on anyone's content — `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` issues `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`.

Both are wired into the delete sequence in `deleteUserTargetsTx` (SQLite lines 96 and 102).

Evidence:
- `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` and `:102` (calls), with the SQL in `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` (`:380-388`) and `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` (`:400-403`); whole thing runs in one `tx` opened at `:30` and committed at `:51`.
- Same logic in the other drivers: `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:383` and `:401`; `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:420` and `:438`.

Confirmation it is **not** a DB-level rule: the `reaction` table schema declares no foreign key and no `ON DELETE CASCADE` — `store/migration/sqlite/0.20/00__reaction.sql:2-9` (and the identical postgres/mysql definitions at `store/migration/postgres/0.20/00__reaction.sql` and `store/migration/mysql/0.20/00__reaction.sql`). `creator_id`/`content_id` are plain columns, so nothing at the database level would remove reactions on user deletion — the explicit Go DELETEs are what do it.
