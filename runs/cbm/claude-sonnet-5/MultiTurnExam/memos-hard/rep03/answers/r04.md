**Premise correction:** Neither. There is no message broker at all — `go.mod` has no NATS or RabbitMQ/AMQP dependency, and a repo-wide search for `nats`/`rabbitmq`/`amqp` in Go source returns nothing.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook delivery uses an in-process, in-memory Go channel as its "queue." It's declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:35`:

```go
asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)
```

A fixed pool of 4 goroutines, spawned in that package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:38-50`), range over `asyncPostQueue` and call `Post` for each dequeued payload. Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184`), which does a non-blocking send and drops (with a logged warning) if the channel's 128-item buffer is full — there's no persistence or cross-process delivery, it's entirely bounded, best-effort, and lost on process restart.
