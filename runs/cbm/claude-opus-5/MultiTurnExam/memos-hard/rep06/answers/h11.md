**1st: `store.User` (~6 packages outside `store/`); 2nd: `store.Memo` (~4 packages)**

Both types are declared in the data layer — `store.User` at `store/user.go`, `store.Memo` at `store/memo.go`.

Referencing packages outside `store/` (non-test files only):

- **`store.User` — 6:** `server/auth`, `server/notification`, `server/router/api/v1`, `server/router/api/v1/test`, `server/router/fileserver`, `server/router/rss`. Discounting `server/router/api/v1/test` as a test-support package, it's 5 production packages. It's also the densest by file count — 16 files in `server/router/api/v1` alone.
- **`store.Memo` — 4:** `server/notification`, `server/router/api/v1`, `server/router/rss`, `server/runner/memopayload` (12 files in `server/router/api/v1`).

The rest of the field: `store.Attachment` — 3 (`server/router/api/v1`, `server/router/fileserver`, `server/router/rss`); `store.MemoShare` — 2; `store.Inbox` — 2; `store.Reaction`, `store.UserSetting`, `store.UserIdentity`, `store.IdentityProvider`, `store.InstanceSetting`, `store.MemoRelation` — 1 each, essentially confined to `server/router/api/v1`.

Method note: I matched `store.<Type>` with a word boundary across all non-test `.go` files, then excluded everything under `store/` — that drops the three driver packages (`store/db/mysql`, `store/db/postgres`, `store/db/sqlite`), which reference every entity type but are part of the data layer itself. The word boundary also keeps `store.User` from absorbing `store.UserSetting`/`store.UserIdentity` hits.
