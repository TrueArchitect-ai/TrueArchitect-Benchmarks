**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this repository — no NATS, no RabbitMQ, no AMQP/Kafka client at all, and therefore no queue declaration.** A case-insensitive search for `nats`, `rabbit`, `amqp`, and `kafka` across every Go file returns zero hits, and `go.mod`/`go.sum` contain no broker dependency.

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed worker pool, entirely inside `internal/webhook/webhook.go`.**

- **The "queue"** is a package-level buffered channel of 128 payloads, declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`:
  ```go
  asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)
  ```

- **The "workers"** are 4 goroutines started in the package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`). Each ranges over the channel and calls `Post` for every payload, logging a warning on failure. Because they're started in `init()`, they spin up on package import rather than being wired into server startup.

- **Enqueue** happens in `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), called from the memo dispatchers at `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`.

Two consequences of using a plain channel rather than a broker are worth noting, since they're the properties a broker would normally provide:

1. **No durability.** The queue lives in process memory; anything pending is lost on restart or crash. There's no persistence, no ack, and no redelivery — `Post` failures are logged (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:47-51`) and dropped, with no retry.
2. **Lossy under backpressure.** `PostAsync` uses a non-blocking `select` with a `default` branch, so once 128 payloads are outstanding, new dispatches are discarded with a "Dropped webhook dispatch because the async queue is full" warning (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:189-195`) rather than blocking the RPC.
