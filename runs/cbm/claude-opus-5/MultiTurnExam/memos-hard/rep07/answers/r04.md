**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker anywhere in this repository — zero references to NATS, RabbitMQ, AMQP, or Kafka across all Go sources, `go.mod`, and YAML configs. No queue is declared because none exists in the external sense.**

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed worker pool, all inside `internal/webhook/webhook.go`.**

- **The "queue"** is a package-level buffered channel holding 128 payloads: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`.
- **The workers** are four goroutines started in the package's `init()` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`. Each ranges over the channel and calls `Post(payload)`, logging a warning on failure. They live for the lifetime of the process; there is no shutdown path that drains or stops them.
- **The producer** is `PostAsync` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`. It does a non-blocking send: if the buffer is full, the `default` branch fires and the delivery is **dropped** with a `slog.Warn` (`:191-195`), not retried or persisted. A nil payload is likewise dropped (`:185-188`).
- **Delivery** happens in `Post` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120` — which marshals the payload, adds Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 headers when a signing secret is configured (`:133-150`), and issues the POST through the SSRF-guarded `safeClient` at `:152`.

Consequences worth being explicit about, since they're the practical difference from a broker: deliveries are **not durable** — anything still in the channel is lost on restart — there is **no retry** on failure or non-2xx response, and the queue is **per-process**, so a multi-replica deployment has one independent 128-slot buffer per instance.

Producers of these payloads are the memo webhook dispatchers at `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`.
