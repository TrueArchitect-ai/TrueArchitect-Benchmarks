**Mechanism: explicit Go code** — reactions are deleted by hand-written `DELETE FROM reaction` statements inside the user-deletion transaction, in every driver. There is no FK/cascade or trigger on `reaction.creator_id`.

## What I checked

**1. The delete path.** `store.DeleteUser` is a thin wrapper that delegates to the driver and then evicts caches — no reaction logic (`store/user.go:171-181`). Each driver implements `DeleteUser` in its own `user_delete.go`, opening one transaction and calling `deleteUserTargetsTx`.

**2. The two explicit reaction deletes.** In `deleteUserTargetsTx` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:92-127`), reactions are removed in two passes, both before the memos and user row are dropped:

- `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:380-388`) — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`, batched at 500. Removes *everyone's* reactions on the departing user's memos. The content IDs come from `memoContentIDsFromRefs`, which rebuilds the `"memos/"+uid` name form (`user_delete.go:512-518`); the memo set itself is a recursive CTE over `memo_relation` of type `COMMENT`, so comment sub-trees are included (`user_delete.go:133-166`).
- `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:400-403`) — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`. Removes the user's own reactions, including ones left on other people's memos.

Postgres and MySQL carry identical logic: `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96,102,380-403` and `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96,102,417-439`.

**3. Ruled out a database-level rule.** The `reaction` table declares only a `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)` constraint — `creator_id` and `content_id` are plain columns with no foreign key:
- `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:91-99`
- `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:90-98`
- `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91-98`

Grepping `REFERENCES` across all three `LATEST.sql` files returns exactly one FK in each — `memo_share.memo_id → memo(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` — and nothing touching `reaction` or `user`. No triggers exist either; the only ones in migration history were modification-time triggers dropped back in 0.7 (`store/migration/sqlite/0.7/01__remove_triggers.sql`). And SQLite runs with FK enforcement disabled outright: `?_pragma=foreign_keys(0)` in `store/db/sqlite/sqlite.go:53`, so a cascade couldn't fire there regardless.

**4. Test coverage confirms the intended semantics.** `server/router/api/v1/test/user_service_delete_test.go:188-298` seeds four reactions and asserts after deletion that reactions on the user's own memo and on their comment are gone, while a third party's reaction on a *foreign* memo survives (`require.Len(t, foreignMemoReactions, 1)`). Since all of this runs in one transaction, a failure rolls the reactions back — verified by the failpoint test at `user_service_delete_test.go:366-431`, which asserts the reaction still exists after an aborted delete.
