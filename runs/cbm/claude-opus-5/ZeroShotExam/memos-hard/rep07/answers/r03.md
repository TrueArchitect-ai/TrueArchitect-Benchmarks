**Premise correction: there is no nightly database-cleanup job in this repository.** The question assumes a scheduled cleanup job exists; it doesn't.

What actually exists:

- `internal/scheduler/` is a standalone, general-purpose cron scheduler *library* (GitHub Actions-inspired, zero external dependencies) — `scheduler.go`, `job.go`, `parser.go`, `middleware.go`. It registers no jobs of its own.
- **Nothing in the application wires it up.** A repo-wide search for imports of `github.com/usememos/memos/internal/scheduler` returns only two hits, both inside the package's own tests: `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:10` and `internal/scheduler/integration_test.go:13`. The server (`server/server.go`) never constructs a scheduler or registers jobs.

The "2 AM cleanup" that likely prompted the question appears only in **documentation and example code**, and it isn't database-related:

- `internal/scheduler/doc.go:15-19` — package doc example: `Name: "daily-cleanup"`, `Schedule: "0 2 * * *"` with a `// Your cleanup logic here` placeholder handler.
- `internal/scheduler/README.md:34-35` — same illustrative snippet, `"0 2 * * *", // 2 AM daily`.
- `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:97-106` — runnable example `Example_multipleJobs` with a `"cleanup"` job at `"0 2 * * *"` whose handler just prints `"Cleaning up old data..."`.
- `internal/scheduler/README.md:240-246` — a naming-conventions example showing a `"stale-session-cleanup"` job at `"0 3 * * *"` (3 AM). Note this is a *different* time from the 2 AM one, which is a good sign these are illustrative rather than a real configuration.

The real cleanup activity in the codebase is not cron-scheduled at all — it's an in-memory cache eviction loop driven by a ticker: `store/cache/cache.go:199-218` (`cleanupLoop`) running on a `CleanupInterval` of 5 minutes (`store/cache/cache.go:60`, `store/store.go:42`). Other "cleanup" references (`store/attachment.go`, `server/router/api/v1/user_service.go:421-450`) are inline, request-triggered resource cleanup, not scheduled jobs.
