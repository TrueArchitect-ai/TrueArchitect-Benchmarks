Reactions are removed by **explicit Go code**, not by a database-level cascade rule. The `deleteUserTargetsTx` function in each database driver performs two separate reaction-deletion steps within the user-deletion transaction:

1. **`deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx`** (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96`, line 380–388) — deletes all reactions whose `content_id` matches the user's memos (i.e., reactions by *anyone* on the deleted user's content): `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`.

2. **`deleteReactionsByCreatorTx`** (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:102`, line 400–403) — deletes all reactions *created by* the deleted user on other people's content: `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`.

The same pattern is present in the Postgres (`store/db/postgres/user_delete.go`) and MySQL (`store/db/mysql/user_delete.go`) drivers. I also verified there are no `ON DELETE CASCADE` or foreign-key constraints on the `reaction` table in the migration files:
