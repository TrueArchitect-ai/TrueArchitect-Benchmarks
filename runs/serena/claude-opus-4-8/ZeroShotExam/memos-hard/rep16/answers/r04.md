**Premise correction:** The question presents a false choice. The webhook delivery workers consume from **neither NATS nor RabbitMQ** — this repository (the `memos` project) has no external message broker at all. There are zero references to `nats`, `jetstream`, `amqp`, `rabbitmq`, or similar in the Go source, `go.mod`, or `go.sum`.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook delivery is entirely **in-process**, using a buffered Go channel plus a fixed pool of goroutines.

- **The "queue"** is a buffered channel declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`:
  ```go
  asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)
  ```
  It has a fixed capacity of 128 — there is no durable broker or declared subject/stream.

- **The "workers"** are 4 goroutines started in `init()` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`, each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post()` (a direct `http.Client` POST at `webhook.go:120`).

- **The producer** is `PostAsync()` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`, called from `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`. If the 128-slot buffer is full, it hits a `default` branch and **drops** the webhook (logged as "Dropped webhook dispatch because the async queue is full"); in-flight items are also lost on process shutdown, since nothing durable backs the channel.

Because producer and consumers share the exact same in-process channel in the same file, no broker mismatch is possible.
