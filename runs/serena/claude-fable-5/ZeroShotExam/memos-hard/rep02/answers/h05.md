**Mechanism: removed by explicit Go code**, inside the user-deletion transaction in each database driver — not by any database-level rule.

What I checked:

1. **The delete-user flow.** `Store.DeleteUser` (`store/user.go:171`) dispatches to per-driver implementations. In each driver's `deleteUserTargetsTx`, reactions are explicitly deleted twice — once for reactions *on* the deleted user's memos (and their comment tree), and once for reactions *made by* the user:
   - SQLite: `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` (`deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx`, defined at line 380, runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`) and line 102 (`deleteReactionsByCreatorTx`, defined at line 400, runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`).
   - Postgres: same pair at `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96`, `:102`, with implementations at `:380` and `:400`.
   - MySQL: same pair at `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96`, `:102`, with implementations at `:417` and `:437`.

2. **The schema, to rule out a database-level rule.** The `reaction` table DDL in all three dialects has no foreign keys at all — just a `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)` constraint: `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91-98`. The only `ON DELETE CASCADE` in each schema belongs to the `memo_share` table (e.g. `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:109`), referencing `memo`, not `user` or `reaction`. So without the explicit Go deletes, reaction rows would be orphaned rather than cascaded.

There's also a test exercising this path at `server/router/api/v1/test/user_service_delete_test.go`.

**Answer:** explicit Go code; `store/db/{sqlite,postgres,mysql}/user_delete.go` (`deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` / `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx`, called from `deleteUserTargetsTx` at lines 96 and 102 in each), with no FK/cascade on the `reaction` table per `store/migration/*/LATEST.sql`.
