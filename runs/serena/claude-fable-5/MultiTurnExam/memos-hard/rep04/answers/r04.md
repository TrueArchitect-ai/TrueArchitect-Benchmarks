**Premise correction:** neither — there is no message broker in this codebase at all. Grepping for NATS/RabbitMQ/AMQP turns up nothing; webhook delivery does not leave the process.

**Actual mechanism:** the "queue" is a plain in-process buffered Go channel. It's declared in `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39` as `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)`. The package's `init()` (`webhook.go:42-55`) starts **4 worker goroutines** that range over this channel and call `Post` (`webhook.go:120`) to execute the actual HTTP delivery. Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking send: if the 128-slot buffer is full, the dispatch is **dropped** with a warning log rather than blocking the caller. So delivery is best-effort, bounded, and entirely in-memory — nothing is persisted or brokered, and pending webhooks are lost on process shutdown.
