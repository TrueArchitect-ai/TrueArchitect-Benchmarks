**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this repository at all** — grepping the Go sources and `go.mod` for `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, `kafka`, `pubsub`, and `sqs` returns zero hits. No external queue is declared anywhere because none is used.

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed pool of goroutines, all in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`.**

- **The "queue"** is a package-level channel declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)`. That's the entire queue declaration — capacity 128, process-local, non-durable.

- **The "workers"** are 4 goroutines started in `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`). Each ranges over the channel and calls `Post` for every payload, logging a warning on failure rather than retrying.

- **Producing** goes through `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), called from the memo dispatchers at `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`. It does a non-blocking send with a `default` branch: when the buffer is full, the payload is **dropped** with a warning (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:191-194`), not backpressured.

- **Delivery** happens in `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`), which signs the body with HMAC-SHA256 Standard Webhooks headers when a secret is configured and issues the POST via `safeClient.Do` at `:152`, with a 30-second timeout (`:27`).

Consequences of this design worth being explicit about, since they're exactly what a broker would change: there is no persistence (a restart loses queued webhooks), no retry on failure, no dead-letter path, no delivery ordering guarantee across the 4 workers, and no cross-replica coordination — each server process has its own independent channel and worker pool.
