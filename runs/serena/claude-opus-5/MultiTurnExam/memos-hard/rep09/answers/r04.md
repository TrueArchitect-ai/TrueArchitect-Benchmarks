**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this repository** — no NATS, no RabbitMQ, no Kafka, no AMQP client of any kind. `go.mod` declares none of them, and grepping the Go sources for `nats`, `rabbitmq`, or `amqp.` returns zero hits. (The single `go.sum` line matching "nats" is a coincidental substring in `github.com/cespare/xxhash/v2`'s hash, not a dependency.) Consequently there is no queue *declaration* in the broker sense — no exchange, subject, stream, or binding.

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel.** Everything lives in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- **The "queue"** is a package-level channel with a fixed capacity of 128:
  `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`.
- **The workers** are 4 goroutines started in `init()`, each ranging over that channel and calling `Post` for every payload, logging a warning on failure — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`.
- **The producer** is `PostAsync`, which does a non-blocking `select` send. If the buffer is full it takes the `default` branch and **drops the webhook** with a warning rather than blocking the caller — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`.
- **Delivery** happens in `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`), which signs the payload and issues `safeClient.Do(req)` at `:152`.

Producers are the memo lifecycle handlers, via `webhook.PostAsync` at `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`.

Practical consequences of this being a channel rather than a broker, worth knowing if you were expecting broker semantics: there is **no durability** (a restart loses everything in flight), **no retry or dead-lettering** (a failed `Post` is logged and discarded), **no cross-process fan-out** (each replica has its own independent queue and workers), and **backpressure is silent loss** at 128 queued items.
