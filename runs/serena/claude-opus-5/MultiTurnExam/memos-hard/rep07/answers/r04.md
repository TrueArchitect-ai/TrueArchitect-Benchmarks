**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this project at all** — searching `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, `kafka`, `sqs`, and `pubsub` across the Go sources, `go.mod`, `go.sum`, and YAML config yields no broker dependency (the sole textual match is an unrelated `xxhash` line in `go.sum`). Nothing is declared, connected to, or consumed from an external queue.

**Actual mechanism: a buffered in-process Go channel with a fixed worker pool, entirely inside `internal/webhook`.**

- **"Queue" declaration**: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. A package-level channel with a 128-slot buffer; no broker, no topic, no exchange.
- **Workers**: four goroutines spawned in `init()` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55` — each ranging over the channel and calling `Post`, logging failures with `slog.Warn`. They start on package import, not via any explicit lifecycle wiring.
- **Producer**: `PostAsync` does a non-blocking send with a `default` branch — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:189-195`. When the buffer is full the payload is **dropped** with a warning, not backpressured or retried.
- **Delivery**: `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`) builds the request, optionally HMAC-signs it, and sends via the SSRF-guarded `safeClient` (`:152`).

Practical consequences worth flagging, since they're exactly what a broker would otherwise provide: the queue is **process-local and in-memory**, so pending webhooks are lost on restart or crash; there is **no persistence, no retry, no dead-letter path, and no delivery guarantee** — a failed `Post` is logged and discarded; and under burst load beyond 128 queued items, events are silently shed at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:191-195`. Concurrency is capped at 4 in-flight deliveries regardless of instance size.
