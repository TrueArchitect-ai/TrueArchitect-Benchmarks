**Mechanism: explicit Go code — two hand-written `DELETE FROM reaction` statements inside the delete-user transaction, in each of the three DB drivers. There is no database-level cascade for reactions.**

## What I checked

**1. The delete-user transaction (the mechanism)**

`DeleteUser` is driver-implemented (`store/driver.go:48`, dispatched from `store/user.go:171`). Each driver has a `user_delete.go` that runs everything in one transaction, and `deleteUserTargetsTx` deletes reactions in two passes — first by the memos being removed, then by the user as author:

- `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96,102` → `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` (line 380: `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`) and `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` (line 400: `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`)
- `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96,102`, bodies at lines 380 and 400 — identical shape
- `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96,102`, bodies at lines 417 and 437

The `content_id` pass matters because `reaction.content_id` is a *string* resource name (`memos/<uid>`), not a memo FK — so reactions other people left on the deleted user's memos could never be cleaned up by a key constraint. It's collected via `memoContentIDsFromRefs` over the recursive memo tree (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:512`, `:129`). Both deletes are batched at 500 (`deleteUserBatchSize`, line 15).

**2. Ruled out a database-level rule**

The `reaction` table declares no foreign key at all in any dialect — just a `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)`:
- `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`
- `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`
- `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91-98`

Grepping `REFERENCES|CASCADE|FOREIGN KEY` across all three `LATEST.sql` files returns exactly one hit per dialect, and it's `memo_share.memo_id → memo(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` (`sqlite:109`, `postgres:108`, `mysql:108`) — nothing touching `reaction`.

**3. Tests confirm the observable behavior**

- `store/test/user_delete_test.go:97-113, 217-220` — the deleted user's reactions go away; a *peer's* reaction on a *peer's* memo survives.
- `server/router/api/v1/test/user_service_delete_test.go:188-209, 287-298` — reactions on the user's own memo and on their comments are empty afterward, while a peer's reaction on a foreign memo is retained.
- `server/router/api/v1/test/user_service_delete_test.go:366-431` — with the `before_commit` failpoint (`store/user_delete.go:14`), the reaction is still present after rollback, confirming the deletes are part of the same transaction rather than a separate step.
