**Explicit Go code — two `DELETE FROM reaction` statements inside the driver-level delete-user transaction. There is no database-level cascade for reactions.**

What I checked, and what each showed:

**1. The delete path.** `store.DeleteUser` (`store/user.go:171`) delegates straight to `s.driver.DeleteUser`, then invalidates caches (`store/user_delete.go:39`). The real work is per-driver in `store/db/{sqlite,postgres,mysql}/user_delete.go`, all three structured identically: `collectDeleteUserTargets` gathers the memo tree, attachments, settings, and inbox IDs, then `deleteUserTargetsTx` issues the deletes in dependency order inside one transaction.

**2. Reactions are deleted explicitly, in two passes.** In `deleteUserTargetsTx`:
- `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` — removes reactions *on* the user's memos, regardless of who reacted. Called at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96`; implemented at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:380`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:380`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:417` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`, chunked.
- `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` — removes reactions the user *left* on other people's memos. Called at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:102`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:102`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:102`; implemented at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:400`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:400`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:437` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`.

Both run before `deleteMemosTx` and `deleteUserRowTx`, so the rows are gone before their referents.

**3. The schema has no rule that would do this.** The `reaction` table is declared at `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91`, and `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91`. In all three it is just `id`, `created_ts`, `creator_id`, `content_id`, `reaction_type`, plus a `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)` — `creator_id` is a plain `INTEGER`/`INT` with no `REFERENCES` clause, and `content_id` is free-form text. Grepping `REFERENCES|ON DELETE` across all three `LATEST.sql` files returns exactly one hit per file, and it's on a different table: `memo_share.memo_id → memo(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:109`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:108`, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:108`). So reactions have no FK and no cascade. (SQLite would also need `PRAGMA foreign_keys=ON` for cascades to fire at all, which is moot here.)

**4. Behavior is pinned by a test.** `TestDeleteUserCleansRelatedData` (`store/test/user_delete_test.go`) creates reactions by the deleted user and by a peer, then asserts that a peer's reaction on an unrelated memo survives while the rest are gone (`store/test/user_delete_test.go:97-112`, `:217-220`) — confirming the two-pass scoping is intentional rather than incidental.
