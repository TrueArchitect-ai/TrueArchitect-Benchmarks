**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this repository — no NATS, no RabbitMQ, and no queue declaration anywhere.** A case-insensitive grep across all Go sources, `go.mod`, and `go.sum` for `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, `kafka`, `streadway`, `sqs`, and `pubsub` produces no real hits (the single `go.sum` match is an accidental substring inside a base64 checksum for `cespare/xxhash`). No broker client is a dependency, so nothing could be consumed from one.

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed worker pool**, entirely inside `internal/webhook/webhook.go`.

- **The "queue"** is a package-level buffered channel holding 128 payloads: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. That line is the closest thing to a queue declaration — it's a local variable, not a broker topic or exchange.
- **The workers** are 4 goroutines started in the package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`). Each ranges over the channel and calls `Post` for every payload, logging a warning on failure.
- **Producers** call `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking `select` send. If the buffer is full, the payload is **silently dropped** with a warning log (`webhook.go:191-195`) — there is no persistence, retry, dead-letter path, or redelivery. The API-layer dispatchers feed it at `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:43` and `:71`.
- **Delivery** happens in `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`), which issues the HTTP POST via the SSRF-guarded `safeClient` at `webhook.go:152`.

Two operational consequences of it being in-process rather than brokered: queued webhooks are lost on process shutdown or crash (nothing drains `asyncPostQueue` during `Server.Shutdown`), and the queue is per-replica — running multiple memos instances gives each its own independent 128-slot buffer and 4 workers.
