**Premise correction:** Neither. There is no external message broker involved in webhook delivery — no NATS and no RabbitMQ (nor Kafka/AMQP). A codebase-wide search finds zero references to `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, or `kafka` in any `.go` file, and `go.mod` declares no such dependency. There is no external queue to declare.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook delivery uses an **in-process, bounded Go channel** as the work queue. It's declared in `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39` (`asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — an in-memory buffered channel of capacity 128). A pool of **4 goroutine workers** is started in that package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post` to perform the HTTP delivery. Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking send and drops the payload (logging a warning) if the channel is full. Because the queue lives in memory, it is not durable across restarts.

Answer: **No broker (not NATS or RabbitMQ); webhook workers consume from an in-memory buffered Go channel `asyncPostQueue` (cap 128) declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`, drained by 4 goroutines started in `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`).**
