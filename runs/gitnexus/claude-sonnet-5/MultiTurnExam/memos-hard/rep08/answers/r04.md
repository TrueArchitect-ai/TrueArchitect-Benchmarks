**Premise correction**: There is no message broker involved — neither NATS nor RabbitMQ appears anywhere in this codebase (`grep -ri "nats\|rabbitmq\|amqp"` across all Go source returns zero hits, and neither is a dependency in `go.mod`).

**Actual mechanism**: Webhook delivery uses a plain in-process, in-memory Go channel. It's declared as `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` in `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. Four worker goroutines are spun up in that package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over the channel and calling `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`) for every payload. `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`) enqueues onto this channel with a non-blocking `select`/`default`, dropping (and logging a warning for) any payload if the 128-slot buffer is full — there's no external broker, persistence, or cross-process queueing involved.
