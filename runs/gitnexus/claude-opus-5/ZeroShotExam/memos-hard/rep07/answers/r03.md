**Premise correction: there is no nightly database-cleanup job in this repository's cron scheduler.** In fact, the cron scheduler isn't wired into the running application at all.

What actually exists:

- `internal/scheduler/` is a self-contained, generic cron-scheduler library (`scheduler.go`, `job.go`, `parser.go`, `middleware.go`). Nothing outside that package imports or uses it — `grep` for `scheduler.` across the repo returns hits only inside `internal/scheduler/` itself (including its own tests). So no job of any kind is registered in production code, and `Scheduler.Register` (`internal/scheduler/scheduler.go:70`) is only ever called from tests and examples.

- The "2 AM cleanup" you may be thinking of is illustrative documentation, not a scheduled job:
  - `internal/scheduler/doc.go:14-20` — package doc example registering `"daily-cleanup"` with `Schedule: "0 2 * * *" // 2 AM daily`, whose handler body is the comment `// Your cleanup logic here`.
  - `internal/scheduler/example_test.go:97-106` — `Example_multipleJobs` registers a `"cleanup"` job at `"0 2 * * *"` whose handler just prints `"Cleaning up old data..."`.
  
  Neither touches the database; both are runnable-doc samples alongside sibling examples like `health-check` at `*/5 * * * *` and `weekly-backup` at `0 1 * * 0`.

The real periodic background work in the app uses plain tickers, not the cron scheduler:

- `server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:27` — S3 presigned-link refresh every 12 hours (`runnerInterval = time.Hour * 12`).
- `store/cache/cache.go:46-60,199-218` — in-memory cache expiry sweep every 5 minutes (`CleanupInterval`), which is process memory, not the database.
- `server/runner/memopayload/` — the other runner (one-shot/on-demand rebuild, not interval-scheduled).
