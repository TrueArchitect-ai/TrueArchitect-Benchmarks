**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker anywhere in this codebase — no NATS, no RabbitMQ, no Kafka, no external queue of any kind. The "workers" consume from an in-process buffered Go channel.**

Verification: case-insensitive fixed-string searches across all `.go` files and `go.mod` return 0 hits for `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, `kafka`, and `pubsub`. (The 11 `nsq` hits are substring noise from identifiers, not the NSQ library — there's no NSQ import either.) The whole webhook subsystem lives in one file, `internal/webhook/webhook.go`, and its only network dependency is `net/http`.

**The actual mechanism** — a package-level fan-out over a bounded channel:

- **"Queue" declaration**: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. A buffered channel with capacity 128, declared in the package's `var` block. Nothing is declared or bound on any broker.
- **Workers**: four goroutines started in `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post(payload)`, logging a warning on failure. They are unnamed, unsupervised, and live for the process lifetime — there's no restart, backoff, or graceful drain on shutdown.
- **Producer**: `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184`) does a non-blocking `select` send at `:190`; if the buffer is full it hits the `default` branch and **drops the event** with a warning (`:191-195`). It also drops a nil payload (`:185-188`).
- **Delivery**: `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`) marshals JSON, optionally adds Standard Webhooks HMAC headers, and sends via `safeClient.Do` (`:152`) with a 30s timeout and the SSRF dial guard.

Consequences worth flagging, since a broker would normally cover them: there is **no persistence, no retry, and no delivery guarantee** — a full buffer, a failed HTTP request, or a process restart silently loses the webhook. Delivery ordering across the 4 workers is also not preserved.
