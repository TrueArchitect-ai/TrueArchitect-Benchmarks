**1st: `store.User` — 6 packages; 2nd: `store.Memo` — 4 packages.**

Method: I enumerated the exported struct types declared in `store/*.go`, then for each counted the distinct directories (Go packages) outside `store/` that reference it, excluding `_test.go` files. Word-boundary matching keeps `store.User` from absorbing `store.UserSetting`/`store.UserIdentity` and `store.Memo` from absorbing `store.MemoRelation`/`store.MemoShare`.

**`store.User`** (`/bench/home/work/store/user.go`) — 6 packages:
- `server/auth`
- `server/notification`
- `server/router/api/v1`
- `server/router/api/v1/test`
- `server/router/fileserver`
- `server/router/rss`

**`store.Memo`** (`/bench/home/work/store/memo.go`) — 4 packages:
- `server/notification`
- `server/router/api/v1`
- `server/router/rss`
- `server/runner/memopayload`

Full ranking of the rest: `store.Attachment` 3 (`server/router/api/v1`, `server/router/fileserver`, `server/router/rss`); `store.Inbox` and `store.MemoShare` 2 each; `store.Reaction`, `store.MemoRelation`, `store.UserSetting`, `store.UserIdentity`, `store.InstanceSetting`, `store.IdentityProvider` 1 each.

Two notes on how to read this:

- `User` leads because it's the identity type threaded through authentication and per-request authorization — `server/auth` and `server/router/fileserver` both need it for access checks even though neither deals with memo content. If you exclude `server/router/api/v1/test` as test-harness code (it isn't a `_test.go` file but is test infrastructure), `User` is 5 and still first.
- These are *package* counts, not reference counts. By raw reference volume the ordering could differ, since `server/router/api/v1` alone accounts for a large share of `store.Memo` usage.
