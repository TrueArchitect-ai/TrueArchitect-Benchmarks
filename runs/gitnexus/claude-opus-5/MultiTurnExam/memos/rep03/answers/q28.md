s3presign.Runner — one immediate RunOnce at startup, then a 12-hour time.Ticker loop

That's the only one. `Server.startBackgroundRunners` (`server/server.go:153-174`) creates `s3presign.NewRunner(s.Store)`, calls `RunOnce(ctx)` synchronously (line 163), then launches `Run(s3Context)` in a goroutine tracked by a `WaitGroup` (lines 166-171). `Run` is a `time.NewTicker(runnerInterval)` loop with `runnerInterval = 12 * time.Hour` (`server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:27-42`), re-signing S3 object URLs whose `LastPresignedTime` is older than 4 days (line 88). Shutdown cancels its dedicated context and waits on the WaitGroup (`server/server.go:140-143, 176-201`).

**Unused scheduling infrastructure:**

- **`server/runner/memopayload.Runner` is never started.** `memopayload.NewRunner` and `Runner.RunOnce` (`server/runner/memopayload/runner.go:19, 27`) — a batched sweep that rebuilds every memo's payload 100 rows at a time — have zero call sites anywhere in the repo, including tests. Only the package-level helper `memopayload.RebuildMemoPayload` (line 74) is used, called inline per-memo from `APIV1Service.CreateMemo` (`server/router/api/v1/memo_service.go:108`) and `UpdateMemo` (line 447). So the payload-rebuild *runner type* is dead scheduling infrastructure; the rebuild *logic* is live on the request path.

Two things that run continuously but aren't runners in this sense: the `SSEHub` (`server/router/api/v1/sse_hub.go`), which is event-driven per subscriber and closed at shutdown via `closeLongLivedConnections` (`server/server.go:203`), and `webhook.PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go`), which spawns a goroutine per dispatch rather than being scheduled.
