Delivery function — `Post` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`; protection mechanism — `safeDialContext` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59` combined with `isReservedIP` / `ValidateURL` at `internal/webhook/validate.go`.

The SSRF protection works at two layers:

1. **At dial time** (`safeDialContext`, `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59-77`): The custom `DialContext` on the shared `safeClient` HTTP transport resolves the target hostname via `net.DefaultResolver.LookupHost`, then checks every resolved IP against `isReservedIP` (`internal/webhook/validate.go:44-54`). This blocks connections to any reserved/private CIDR — loopback, RFC-1918, link-local (including cloud IMDS at `169.254.169.254`), and IPv6 equivalents — at connect time, which also defeats DNS rebinding attacks.

2. **At registration time** (`ValidateURL`, `internal/webhook/validate.go:62-83`): When a webhook URL is first configured, it is validated to parse correctly, use only `http`/`https` schemes, and not resolve to a reserved IP. This provides an early rejection before any delivery is attempted.

The reserved CIDRs are defined in `internal/webhook/validate.go:14-23` and parsed into `*net.IPNet` at startup. An `AllowPrivateIPs` flag (`internal/webhook/validate.go:41`) exists to let self-hosted deployments opt out of the SSRF protection when webhooks intentionally target local-network services.
