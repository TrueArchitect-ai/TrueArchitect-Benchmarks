**Premise correction: neither — there is no message broker at all.** Searching the Go sources and `go.mod` for NATS, RabbitMQ/AMQP (or Kafka) turns up nothing.

Actual mechanism: webhook delivery is handled entirely in-process in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- The "queue" is a plain buffered Go channel: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)`, declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`.
- The "workers" are 4 goroutines started in the package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over the channel and calling `Post` (`webhook.go:120`) to perform the actual HTTP delivery.
- Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking send; if the 128-slot buffer is full the dispatch is **dropped** with a warning log rather than persisted or retried.

So delivery is best-effort, in-memory, and non-durable — payloads don't survive a process restart, and there's no broker, queue declaration, or acknowledgment semantics anywhere.
