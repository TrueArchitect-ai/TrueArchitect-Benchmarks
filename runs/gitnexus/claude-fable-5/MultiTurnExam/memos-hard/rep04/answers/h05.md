**Mechanism: explicit Go code** — each database driver's user-deletion transaction runs explicit `DELETE FROM reaction` statements. There is no database-level rule for reactions (the `reaction` table has no foreign keys, so no `ON DELETE CASCADE` can apply to it).

What I checked:

1. **The Go deletion code.** All three drivers delete reactions inside the delete-user transaction, in two ways: by creator (`deleteReactionsByCreatorTx`, `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`) and for reactions left by *anyone* on the deleted user's memos (`deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx`, `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`):
   - `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:383` and `:401`
   - `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:420` and `:438`
   - `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:383` and `:401`

   The orchestration lives in `store/user_delete.go` (driver-agnostic `DeleteUser` wrapper with transaction/failpoint support).

2. **The schema, to rule out a DB-level rule.** In all three `LATEST.sql` schemas the `reaction` table (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`, mysql equivalent) has plain `creator_id INTEGER NOT NULL` and `content_id TEXT NOT NULL` columns with **no** `FOREIGN KEY`/`REFERENCES` clause. The only `ON DELETE CASCADE` in the schemas belongs to `memo_share.memo_id → memo(id)` (e.g. `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:109`), not to reactions — so the database cannot clean reactions up on its own, and the explicit Go statements are what does it.
