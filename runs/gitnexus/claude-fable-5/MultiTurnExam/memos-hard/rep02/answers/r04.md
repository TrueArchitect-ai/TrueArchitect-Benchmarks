**Premise correction: neither — there is no message broker at all. No NATS, RabbitMQ (AMQP), Kafka, or any other broker client appears in `go.mod` or anywhere in the Go code.**

Actual mechanism: webhook delivery is queued entirely in-process using a buffered Go channel. In `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- The "queue" is declared at `webhook.go:39`: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — a 128-slot buffered channel, package-level, in memory only.
- The "workers" are 4 goroutines started in the package's `init()` (`webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over the channel and calling `Post` (`webhook.go:120`) to perform the actual signed HTTP POST through the SSRF-guarded client.
- Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking send; if the channel is full the dispatch is **dropped** with a warning log rather than persisted.

Consequences of this design worth noting: there is no durability, retry, or cross-process fan-out — queued webhooks are lost on process shutdown or when the buffer overflows, which is exactly the trade-off a broker would change.
