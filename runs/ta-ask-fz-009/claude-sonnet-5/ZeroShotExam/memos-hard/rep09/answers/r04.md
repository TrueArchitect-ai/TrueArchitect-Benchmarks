**Premise correction:** Neither. There's no NATS or RabbitMQ in this codebase — webhook delivery isn't backed by an external message broker at all.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook dispatch uses an in-process Go channel as a bounded work queue. In `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`, `asyncPostQueue` is declared as `make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — a buffered channel of capacity 128, not a broker-backed queue. On package `init()` (webhook.go:42-55), 4 goroutines are spawned that range over this channel and call `Post(payload)` for each item — these are the "delivery workers." `PostAsync` (webhook.go:184-196) enqueues payloads via a non-blocking `select`, dropping and logging a warning if the channel is full rather than blocking.

So the "queue" is declared and consumed entirely in-process in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`; there's no NATS subject, RabbitMQ exchange/queue declaration, or any external broker dependency involved (a parallel pattern exists for email dispatch via `email.asyncEmailQueue` in `internal/email/email.go:14`, same in-memory-channel style).
