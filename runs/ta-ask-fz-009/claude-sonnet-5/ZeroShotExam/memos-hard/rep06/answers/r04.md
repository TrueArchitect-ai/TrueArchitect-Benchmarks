Premise correction: neither. This codebase (usememos/memos) has no NATS or RabbitMQ integration at all — there's no message-broker dependency anywhere in the repo (confirmed via a repo-wide search for "nats"/"rabbitmq"/"amqp", zero hits, and the code graph shows no such component).

Actual mechanism: webhook delivery isn't broker-backed — it's an in-process Go channel used as a bounded worker queue. In `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:
- `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` (line 39) declares the "queue" — a buffered channel of capacity 128.
- `init()` (lines 42–55) spawns 4 goroutines that range over `asyncPostQueue`, calling `Post(payload)` for each item — these are the "delivery workers."
- `PostAsync` (lines 184–196) is the producer side: it does a non-blocking `select` send into `asyncPostQueue`, dropping (with a warning log) if the channel is full rather than blocking.

So delivery is entirely in-memory/in-process, not backed by any external broker; there's no queue durability across restarts and no NATS/RabbitMQ topology to speak of.
