### Where delivery executes

`Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`) marshals the payload, builds the POST, optionally signs it with HMAC-SHA256 in Standard Webhooks form (`webhook-id` / `webhook-timestamp` / `webhook-signature`, lines 133–150), and issues the request at line 152.

Callers never invoke it directly on the request path. `PostAsync` (line 184) enqueues onto `asyncPostQueue`, a buffered channel of 128 (line 39), drained by 4 worker goroutines started in `init()` (lines 42–55) — those workers are what actually call `Post`. The queue is lossy by design: a full queue drops the dispatch with a warning rather than blocking the caller (lines 189–195).

Triggers are in `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go` — `DispatchMemoCommentCreatedWebhook` (line 30) and `dispatchMemoRelatedWebhook` (line 48).

### What protects it

Two layers, both funneling through `isReservedIP` (`internal/webhook/validate.go:44`) against 8 parsed CIDRs (lines 14–23): IPv4 loopback, all three RFC-1918 ranges, link-local `169.254.0.0/16` (explicitly called out as covering cloud IMDS at 169.254.169.254), and the IPv6 equivalents `::1/128`, `fc00::/7`, `fe80::/10`.

1. **Admission check** — `ValidateURL` (`validate.go:62`) rejects non-absolute URLs, non-http(s) schemes, and hostnames resolving into a reserved range, returning a gRPC `InvalidArgument`. Enforced at registration time in `CreateUserWebhook` (`server/router/api/v1/user_service_webhooks.go:53`) and `UpdateUserWebhook` (line 106).

2. **Dial-time check** — the decisive one. `safeClient` (`webhook.go:32`) is the shared client for *all* dispatches, and its `Transport.DialContext` is `safeDialContext` (line 59), which re-resolves the host and refuses the connection if any returned address is reserved (lines 65–74).

The second layer exists because the first is insufficient alone: validation happens once at registration, so an attacker controlling DNS could point a previously-clean hostname at 127.0.0.1 afterward. Re-resolving at connect time is what defeats that DNS rebinding — the code comment at lines 30–31 states this intent explicitly.

### Escape hatch

`AllowPrivateIPs` (`internal/webhook/validate.go:41`) short-circuits `isReservedIP` to `false`, disabling *both* layers at once. It's operator-controlled via the `allow-private-webhooks` flag, bound in `cmd/memos/main.go:49`, and intended for self-hosted setups whose webhook targets genuinely live on the LAN. Default is `false`, so the guard is on unless deliberately switched off.
