**Two structurally different enforcement styles, worth separating:**

*Row-level gates* — fetch the memo, then decide: `memo_service.go:55`, `attachment_service.go:445`, `memo_attachment_service.go:214`, `reaction_service.go:30`, `memo_service_comments.go:41`, `fileserver.go:677`. These share a near-identical three-step shape: allow if `Public`; else require authentication; else deny if `Private` and not creator and not superuser.

*Query-level scoping* — never load invisible rows at all, by setting `memoFind.VisibilityList`: `memo_service.go:219-225`, `user_service_stats.go:84-90` and `:218-220`, `frontend.go:150` (SSR path, hard-coded to `{store.Public}` only).

**The duplication is the risk here.** The same predicate is hand-written in at least six places, and they are *not* uniform. Compare `fileserver.go:699` — `user.Role != store.RoleAdmin` — against the API layer's `!isSuperUser(user)` (`attachment_service.go:451`). Two different spellings of "privileged," in two packages, guarding the same attachment bytes. There's no single `canView(user, memo)` helper; the invariant lives in convention.

**Two sites enforce visibility outside the request path**, which is easy to miss: `SSEHub` filters `Private` memos out of real-time fan-out (`sse_hub.go:136-138`), and `notification/email.go:313-316` plus `user_service_notifications.go:309-312` redact memo content in outbound email by visibility. A leak there wouldn't surface in any API test.

Note `PROTECTED` is only *explicitly* named in the scoping and notification sites; the row-level gates treat it implicitly — anything not `Public` requires auth, anything not `Private` passes once authenticated. Correct today, but it means adding a fourth visibility level would require auditing all twelve sites.
