**Premise correction: neither. There is no message broker in this codebase — no NATS, no RabbitMQ, no Kafka, no AMQP client of any kind — and therefore no queue declaration to point to.**

**Evidence:** a case-insensitive repo-wide search for `nats|rabbitmq|amqp|kafka|streadway|broker` returns **zero code matches** — the only hits are the word "broker" in unrelated SSO design prose (`docs/plans/2026-04-21-sso-user-identity-linkage/design.md:17,38`, discussing Keycloak as an *identity* broker) and coincidental substrings in Catalan translations. This is consistent with `go.mod`'s 142 dependencies, which contain no messaging client (I enumerated them earlier in this session).

**Actual mechanism: an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed worker pool.** Everything lives in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- **"Queue" declaration** — `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. A package-level channel, capacity 128, holding Go pointers — not serialized messages.
- **Workers** — a `package init()` spawns exactly **4 goroutines**, each ranging over the channel and calling `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`). No consumer groups, no acks, no subscriptions.
- **Producer** — `PostAsync` does a non-blocking `select` send; on a full buffer it takes the `default` branch and **drops the delivery** with a warning log (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:189-195`).

The same pattern is used for email — `asyncEmailQueue` at `internal/email/email.go:14`.

**Three architectural consequences of channel-not-broker, worth being explicit about, since they're the properties people usually assume a broker provides:**

1. **No durability.** The queue is process memory. A restart or crash loses every in-flight webhook; there is no persisted outbox.
2. **No retry, no dead-letter.** `Post` failures are logged and discarded (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:46-51`) — a receiver returning 500 gets one attempt, ever.
3. **Silent shedding under load.** Past 128 queued payloads, deliveries are dropped rather than backpressured, so a slow endpoint can cause loss for unrelated webhooks. Note also that unlike the S3 and SSE subsystems, these four workers are *not* registered with `Server.backgroundRunnerWG` — they're started by `init()`, not `startBackgroundRunners`, so `Shutdown` (`server/server.go:134`) does not drain them.

If broker semantics are a requirement for your deployment, none of the guarantees exist today; the insertion point would be `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184`), which is the single producer boundary.
