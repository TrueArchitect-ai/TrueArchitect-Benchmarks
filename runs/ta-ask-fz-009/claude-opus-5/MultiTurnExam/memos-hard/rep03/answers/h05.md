**Explicit Go code — not a database rule.** Reactions are deleted by two hand-written `DELETE` statements per dialect, inside the user-deletion transaction. No FK cascade exists on the `reaction` table in any dialect.

### What I checked

**1. The Go deletion path.** `deleteUserTargetsTx` orchestrates the cascade and calls two separate reaction deletions — `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` and `:102`, mirrored at `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96`/`:102` and `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96`/`:102`. All three dialects implement the same pair:

- `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:380-383`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:417-420`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:380-383`). This removes **everyone's** reactions on the departing user's memos.
- `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` — `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:400-401`, `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:437-438`, `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:400-401`). This removes the user's own reactions on **other people's** memos.

Two queries are needed because `reaction` is reachable by two independent paths; a `creator_id` cascade alone would orphan reactions left by others on deleted memos. The whole set runs in one transaction, committed at `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:51`, so reaction cleanup is atomic with the user removal.

**2. The schema — no cascade to rely on.** The `reaction` table as currently defined has a primary key and one `UNIQUE(creator_id, content_id, reaction_type)` constraint, and nothing else: `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:92-99`, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:91-98`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:91-98`. `creator_id` and `content_id` are plain `INTEGER`/`TEXT` columns with no `REFERENCES` clause.

**3. Grepped every FK in the migration tree** (`FOREIGN KEY|REFERENCES|ON DELETE` across `store/migration/**/*.sql`). Every hit belongs to another table — `memo_share.memo_id` in all three dialects (`LATEST.sql:108`/`:109`, plus `0.27/04__memo_share.sql:11`), and legacy SQLite-only constraints under `0.3/`, `0.4/`, `0.5/`. **No FK on `reaction` appears anywhere.** Note also that the historical cascades in `store/migration/sqlite/0.5/00__regenerate_foreign_keys.sql` are SQLite-only and don't cover reactions either, so there's no dialect where the DB would do this work.

### Why this matters
The invariant is maintained purely in application code, and it's duplicated three times. A fourth driver, or a direct `DELETE FROM user` outside `DeleteUser`, would silently leave orphaned reaction rows — the database will not catch it. Content-ID matching is also string-based (`content_id` is `TEXT`/`VARCHAR(256)`, not an FK), so correctness depends on `memoContentIDsFromRefs` (`store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:94`) producing exactly the right identifiers.
