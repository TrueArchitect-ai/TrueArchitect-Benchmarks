**`s3presign.Runner` (`server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:16`) — one immediate pass at boot, then every 12 hours — wired at `server/server.go:162`** (started from `Server.Start` via `startBackgroundRunners` at `server/server.go:129`).

**What it does.** Attachments with `StorageType == S3` are stored externally and their `Reference` field holds a presigned GET URL, which `internal/storage/s3/s3.go:77` issues with a 5-day expiry. Left alone, those references would go dead. The runner walks all S3-backed attachments in batches of 100 (`store.ListAttachments` with `GetBlob: false`, `runner.go:60-65`), and for each one:

- skips it if `payload.S3Object.LastPresignedTime` is less than 4 days old — a one-day safety margin against the 5-day expiry (`runner.go:85-91`);
- otherwise re-presigns via `s3Client.PresignGetObject` and writes back both the new `Reference` and a fresh `LastPresignedTime` (`runner.go:108-123`).

Per-attachment `S3Config` overrides the instance-level storage setting when present (`runner.go:93-96`), so attachments uploaded under an older bucket config keep being signed with their original credentials.

**Schedule mechanics.** `runnerInterval = time.Hour * 12` (`runner.go:27`); `Run` is a plain `time.Ticker` loop that exits on `ctx.Done()` (`runner.go:29-41`). Note the ticker fires *after* the first 12 hours, so startup freshness comes from the separate synchronous `RunOnce(ctx)` call at `server/server.go:163` — that one runs on the server's own context, before the goroutine is launched.

**Lifecycle boundary.** The loop gets its own cancellable context (`s3Context`, `server.go:156`) whose cancel is registered in `s.backgroundRunnerCancels` and whose goroutine is tracked in `s.backgroundRunnerWG` (`server.go:159`, `166-171`), so `Shutdown` can stop it and wait for it deterministically (`server.go:140-143`).
