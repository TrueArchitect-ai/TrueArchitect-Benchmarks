So there is no external queue to declare and no broker connection to configure.

**Actual mechanism — an in-process buffered Go channel with a fixed worker pool:**

- **Queue declaration:** `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`. A package-level buffered channel, capacity 128. That is the entire "queue."
- **Workers:** `func init()` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55` spawns exactly 4 goroutines, each ranging over `asyncPostQueue` and calling `Post(payload)` per item (line 46). Failures are logged via `slog.Warn` and dropped — no retry, no dead-letter path.
- **Producer:** `PostAsync` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`. It uses a `select` with a `default` branch, so when the buffer is full the payload is **discarded** with a "Dropped webhook dispatch because the async queue is full" warning (lines 191–195). Non-blocking enqueue, lossy under load.
- **Delivery:** `Post` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`. Plain `net/http` POST through `safeClient` (line 32), which installs `safeDialContext` (line 59) to reject reserved/private IPs at dial time as SSRF/DNS-rebinding protection. Optional HMAC-SHA256 signing follows the Standard Webhooks header convention: `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature: v1,<sig>` (lines 147–149).
- **Callers:** only two, both in `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go` — `DispatchMemoCommentCreatedWebhook` (line 30) and `dispatchMemoRelatedWebhook` (line 48).

Architecturally the consequence is worth naming: because the queue is an in-memory channel scoped to the process, it has no durability and no cross-instance semantics. Queued webhooks are lost on restart, and the design cannot fan out across replicas — each process would dispatch its own events independently. That's the tradeoff a broker would have bought.
