Found it: `server/runner/s3presign`. Now the schedule and startup wiring.

h01.

**`s3presign.Runner` (`server/runner/s3presign/runner.go:16`) — every 12 hours, plus one immediate pass at boot — wired at `server/server.go:162-171` (inside `startBackgroundRunners`, itself called from `Server.Start` at `server/server.go:129`).**

**What it does.** S3-stored attachments are served to clients as presigned URLs, which expire. The runner walks all attachments with `StorageType == AttachmentStorageType_S3` in batches of 100 (`runner.go:55-65`), and for each one re-presigns the object key and writes the fresh URL back into `Attachment.Reference` along with a new `LastPresignedTime` on the payload (`runner.go:108-120`).

**Schedule.**
- Interval: `const runnerInterval = time.Hour * 12` (`runner.go:27`), driven by a `time.Ticker` in `Run` with `ctx.Done()` as the exit (`runner.go:29-41`).
- Startup: `RunOnce(ctx)` is called synchronously before the loop goroutine starts (`server/server.go:163`), so stale links are repaired immediately rather than up to 12 hours later.

**The freshness invariant.** Presigned URLs are minted with a 5-day expiry (`internal/storage/s3/s3.go:77`), and the runner skips any attachment whose `LastPresignedTime` is under 4 days old (`runner.go:85-91`). That leaves a 1-day safety margin against a 12-hour tick — the link is always renewed well before it can expire.

**Lifecycle wiring.** The runner gets its own cancellable context (`s3Context`, `server/server.go:156`), its cancel func is registered on `s.backgroundRunnerCancels` (`:159`), and it's tracked on `s.backgroundRunnerWG` (`:166-171`) so `Shutdown` can stop it via `stopBackgroundRunners` and then `waitBackgroundRunners` (`server/server.go:140-143`).

One note: the same presign logic exists on the read path too — `FileServerService.getS3PresignedURL` (`server/router/fileserver/fileserver.go:408-417`) presigns on demand and redirects, so the runner is the background keep-warm layer, not the only source of valid URLs.
