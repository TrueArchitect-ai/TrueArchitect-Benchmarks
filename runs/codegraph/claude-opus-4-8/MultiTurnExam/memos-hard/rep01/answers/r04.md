**Premise correction:** Neither. The webhook delivery workers do not consume from any external message broker — there is no NATS, no RabbitMQ, and no AMQP anywhere in the codebase. A search of the Go source and `go.mod` for `nats`, `rabbitmq`, `amqp`, and `streadway` returns zero matches. There is no external queue to declare.

**Actual mechanism:** the "queue" is an **in-process, in-memory buffered Go channel** with a fixed pool of goroutine workers, all in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:
- The queue is declared as a package-level buffered channel of capacity 128: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`.
- The workers are 4 goroutines started in `init()`, each ranging over that channel and calling `Post` for every payload — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`.
- Producers enqueue via `PostAsync`, which does a non-blocking send onto the channel and drops the event (with a warning) if the buffer is full — `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`. Callers are the memo webhook dispatchers in `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go` (see h06).

Because the channel lives in the process memory, the buffer is not durable — pending webhooks are lost on restart, and there is no broker connection, exchange, or queue declaration.

Answer: **Neither NATS nor RabbitMQ — no message broker exists. Webhook delivery uses an in-memory buffered Go channel `asyncPostQueue` (cap 128) declared at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`, drained by 4 worker goroutines started in `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), fed by `PostAsync` (`:184`).**
