h05.

**Mechanism: removed by explicit Go code** (raw `DELETE FROM reaction` SQL run inside the user-delete transaction), not by a database-level cascade rule.

When a user is deleted, `deleteUserTargetsTx` explicitly deletes their reactions in two ways, within the same transaction:

1. **By creator** — `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`, removing reactions the deleted user authored (including reactions they placed on *other* users' memos).
2. **By content** — `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` runs `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)` for the deleted user's memos, removing reactions *others* left on that user's content.

This is duplicated across all three backends.

Evidence locations (identical logic per driver):
- `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go` — call sites at lines 96 & 102 in `deleteUserTargetsTx`; implementations `deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx` (line 380, `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN ...` at 383) and `deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` (line 400, `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ...`).
- `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go` — call sites at lines 96 & 102; implementations at lines 417 and 437–438.
- `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go` — call sites at lines 96 & 102; implementations at lines 380–383 and 400–401.

What I checked to rule out a DB-level rule: the deletion is driven by application code in `DeleteUser` → `collectDeleteUserTargets` → `deleteUserTargetsTx` (`store/db/*/user_delete.go`). The reaction removal is manual SQL there rather than relying on a foreign-key `ON DELETE CASCADE` — consistent with the fact that the `reaction` table's `content_id` is a UID string (not an FK to memo id) and that reactions-by-creator must also be swept even when the reactions live on memos that aren't being deleted. The standalone `DeleteReaction` driver methods (`store/db/*/reaction.go`) only delete by reaction `id` and are unrelated to user deletion.
