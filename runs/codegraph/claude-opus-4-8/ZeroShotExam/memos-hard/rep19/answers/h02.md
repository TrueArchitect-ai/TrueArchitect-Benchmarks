**Delivery function** — `Post` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`. This is where the outbound HTTP POST actually executes: it marshals the payload, builds the request, adds the optional HMAC signature headers, and fires it with `safeClient.Do(req)` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:152`. (`PostAsync` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:184` just enqueues onto a bounded channel; four background workers started in `init` call `Post` to do the real delivery. Callers such as `dispatchMemoRelatedWebhook` in `server/router/api/v1/memo_service_webhooks.go:48` feed it.)

**Protection mechanism** — `safeDialContext` at `internal/webhook/webhook.go:59`, wired into the shared `safeClient`'s `http.Transport.DialContext` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:32-37`). At dial time it resolves the target host via `net.DefaultResolver.LookupHost` and rejects the connection if any resolved IP satisfies `isReservedIP` (i.e. falls in a reserved/private range). Because the check happens on the *resolved* address at dial time, it also defeats DNS-rebinding attempts. The `isReservedIP` helper lives in `internal/webhook/` (referenced from `safeDialContext`).

So: an attacker cannot point a webhook URL at `127.0.0.1`, `10.x`, `169.254.x`, etc.—the dialer refuses those connections regardless of the user-supplied URL.
