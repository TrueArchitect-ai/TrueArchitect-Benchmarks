**Premise correction: neither.** There is no message broker of any kind in this codebase — `go.mod` contains no NATS, RabbitMQ/AMQP, or Kafka dependency, and no Go source references one. There is consequently no queue "declared" on any broker.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook delivery is queued entirely in-process, in `internal/webhook/webhook.go`:

- The "queue" is a buffered Go channel: `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:39`).
- The "delivery workers" are 4 goroutines started in the package's `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`), each ranging over the channel and calling `Post` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:120`) to perform the actual HTTP POST through the SSRF-guarded client.
- Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`) with a non-blocking `select`: if the 128-slot buffer is full, the dispatch is **dropped** with a warning log rather than blocking or being persisted.

Implications of this design versus a real broker: no durability (queued webhooks are lost on process exit), no retries, and backpressure is handled by dropping events — all deliberate trade-offs for a self-hosted single-binary app.
