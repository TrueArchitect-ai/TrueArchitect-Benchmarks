**Mechanism: explicit Go code inside the DeleteUser transaction — not a DB-level cascade.** Reactions are removed in two sweeps: those *authored by* the deleted user, and those *on* the deleted user's memos (matched by `content_id = "memos/<uid>"`).

**Evidence**

Explicit deletes (same structure in all three drivers, run inside the delete-user transaction in `deleteUserTargetsTx`):
- `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:96` (`deleteReactionsByContentIDsTx`, defined at `:380`-ish → `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE content_id IN (...)`) and `:102` (`deleteReactionsByCreatorTx` → `DELETE FROM reaction WHERE creator_id = ?`)
- `store/db/postgres/user_delete.go:96, 102`; bodies at `:380` and `:400`
- `store/db/mysql/user_delete.go:96, 102`; bodies at `:417` and `:437`
- Content IDs come from `memoContentIDsFromRefs` (`"memos/" + memo.UID`) over the recursive memo tree collected in `listDeleteUserMemoTree`, so reactions on the user's memos *and* on comment memos under them are covered.
- Entry point: `store/user.go:171` → driver `DeleteUser`; whole thing is one `tx` committed at the end (e.g. `store/db/sqlite/user_delete.go:29-57`).

No database-level rule exists:
- The `reaction` table is declared with **no foreign key and no `ON DELETE` clause** in any dialect: `store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:91-99`, `store/migration/mysql/LATEST.sql:90-98`, `store/migration/postgres/LATEST.sql:90-98` (same in the original `store/migration/*/0.20/00__reaction.sql`).
- Grepping `foreign key|references|on delete` across all three `LATEST.sql` files returns exactly one hit each — `memo_share.memo_id REFERENCES memo(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` (`store/migration/sqlite/LATEST.sql:109`, `mysql:108`, `postgres:108`) — nothing touching `reaction`.

Covered by test: `store/test/user_delete_test.go:97-113` seeds three reactions (peer→user's memo, user→peer's memo, peer→peer's memo) and `:216-220` asserts only the third survives.
