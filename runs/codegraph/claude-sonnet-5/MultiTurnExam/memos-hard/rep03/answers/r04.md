**Premise correction:** Neither. There's no message broker involved at all — a repo-wide search for "nats"/"rabbitmq"/"amqp" turns up nothing in the Go source.

**Actual mechanism:** Webhook delivery workers consume from an **in-process Go channel**, not an external broker. `internal/webhook/webhook.go:39` declares `asyncPostQueue = make(chan *WebhookRequestPayload, 128)` — a buffered channel with capacity 128. `init()` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:42-55`) spawns 4 goroutines that range over this channel and call `Post(payload)` for each item. Producers enqueue via `PostAsync` (`internal/webhook/webhook.go:184-196`), which does a non-blocking `select` send and drops the payload with a warning log if the channel is full — there's no persistence, retry queue, or external broker backing it; it's purely an in-memory worker pool scoped to a single process.
